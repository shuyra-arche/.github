# A Design for Coherence

[Read in Chinese](manifesto.zh-CN.md)

## Manifesto

We cannot fully comprehend reality, and so we claim no final answers.

We build to understand, test to discover the limits of our understanding, and reconstruct to move beyond them.

Through clear boundaries, we establish safety. Through free composition, we create order. Through continued questioning, we preserve the possibility of revision.

We value what we have built without treating it as unquestionable truth. We allow errors to be discovered, designs to be challenged, and new understanding to replace old structures.

No construction should be immutable merely because it exists. No understanding should be abandoned merely because it is incomplete.

**We do not demand eternal order. We demand the freedom to create it.**

---

## Understanding Has Limits

We cannot foresee every problem or guarantee that a model will remain correct in every circumstance.

This is not a reason to stop reasoning or acting. We can make the best judgments available to us while remaining willing to discover where they fail.

A conclusion can be useful without being final. An explanation can be meaningful without being complete.

## Building Is a Way of Knowing

We form hypotheses, make models, and build things that can be examined.

Construction puts our understanding into contact with constraints we may not have anticipated. It can reveal new possibilities, expose contradictions, and make hidden assumptions visible.

A failed design need not make the effort meaningless. Understanding why it fails can be a result worth preserving.

**We build not because we already understand, but also because we want to understand.**

## Questioning Without Restlessness

We can be wrong. Neither authority, tradition, nor the effort already invested should make an idea immune to examination.

But change is not automatically progress. We do not reconstruct merely for novelty, or discard reliable work simply because it is old.

A worthwhile revision should improve understanding, reliability, or freedom to explore. We preserve the ability to challenge our conclusions and accept the responsibility to test what replaces them.

## Boundaries and Composition

Freedom does not require the absence of boundaries.

Without clear responsibility, one part can compromise another. Without reliable contracts, an apparently open system may become a network of hidden dependencies.

We seek boundaries that allow parts to remain independent, cooperate safely, and be recomposed when needed.

The aim is not to determine every possible arrangement beforehand. It is to make new arrangements possible without sacrificing safety.

**We establish boundaries to make freedom of creation practical.**

## Coherence and Change

Coherence is not uniformity, and it is not permanence.

We seek structures whose responsibilities, assumptions, and limitations can be understood together. We prefer explanations that make relationships clear over exceptions that merely conceal contradictions.

When experience challenges a model, we reconsider it. When a design is reliable, we do not need to disturb it to prove our openness to change.

Consistency matters because it helps us understand, verify, and responsibly revise what we build.

## Evidence and Responsibility

We distinguish hypotheses from results, aspirations from achievements, and unverified properties from guarantees.

We want claims that can be examined, failures that can be explained, and decisions whose reasons are visible.

When we do not know, we can say so. When something fails, correcting the problem matters more than defending the status of its author.

The freedom to change something comes with responsibility for the effects of that change.

## Open to Revision

No one person's understanding can exhaust a question. An idea should not be accepted or dismissed merely because of who proposed it.

We hope to make room for disagreement grounded in evidence, for independent thought, and for people who may eventually explain our work better than we can.

This statement is no exception to its own principles. It too may be revised.

---

We do not need final answers to offer meaningful ones. We do not need certainty that a design will last forever to build it with care.

**We do not require certainty before creation, nor permanence to give creation meaning.**

We build. We understand. We revise.

And we build again.
